<p>Change always comes with adaptation. <strong>Should people adapt or should places adapt?</strong></p>
<p>We asked ourselves this while analyzing <strong>two corners</strong>: <em>Kronenhalle Bar</em> by Trix and Robert Haussmann in Zurich and <em>Kleines Caf&eacute;</em> by Hermann Czech in Vienna. Both took their initial inspiration from <em>Adolf Loos's American Bar</em>, yet the outcome could hardly be more different. The <em>Kronenhalle Bar</em> appears unchanged, a place frozen in 1965, yet it evolved from a gathering place for everyone into an exclusive and luxurious venue. <em>Kleines Caf&eacute;</em>, by contrast, grew in an iterative, bottom-up manner, conveying a sense of improvisation and even chaos.<span class="Apple-converted-space">&nbsp;</span></p>
<p>Architecture didn't determine these places, yet the culture and context had their influence. Both share a <strong>domestic scale</strong>, but cater to entirely <strong>different social classes</strong>.</p>
<p><em>The first two models and axonometries</em> create a specific atmosphere through their unification: one feels introverted and enclosed, while the other, with its mirrored surfaces and doubled scale, feels open and expansive. The alignment of the entrances forms a "corridor bar", showing that both stem from the same initial inspiration.</p>
<p>From this research grew our proposal: to see the two spaces together.<em> The third model,</em> a "combined corner", is accompanied by an axonometry focusing on the atmosphere each bar creates through architectural elements alone: light, materials, colors, views, and movement. When you turn it around, the perspective shifts in the middle, revealing two different ways of seeing the same space. Through the open doors of Kleines Caf&eacute; you immediately feel welcome, but through the tilted mirror you notice a darker, more closed-off space, one you can see but not reach: Kronenhalle Bar, a place reserved for the upper class or for regular guests who know how to enter and what lies behind the walls.</p>
