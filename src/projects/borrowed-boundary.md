<p class="p1"><span class="s1">As the first phase of an ongoing project, we started with the question:<br><strong>When does light become safety light?</strong></span></p>
<p class="p1"><span class="s1">Our site in Schlieren holds the warehouses of the food distributor Transgourmet: storage on one side, offices on the other, and a steel car park connecting them, with truck dispatch and maintenance on the ground floor. The building is strongly divided by actors and scale. On the ground floor everything is <strong>truck-sized</strong>, with no sidewalks or lights for pedestrians, and only higher up does it become human-sized again.&nbsp;</span></p>
<p class="p1"><span class="s1">Our myth is the <strong>forklift safety light</strong>. Starting from it, we researched and built a storyboard, and concluded that light becomes safety light when it stops only illuminating and starts <strong>communicating</strong>, through colour, intensity and the position of the observer.</span></p>
<p class="p1"><span class="s1">To visualise this research, we created a fragment: a <strong>performance with lasers</strong>, exploring how light reacts to different situations.</span></p>
<ul>
<li><span class="s1"><strong>Light and environment:</strong> light travels as tiny signals, photons, invisible to the human eye unless the room is dusty or foggy. The environment changes how light is perceived.</span></li>
</ul>
<ul class="ul1">
<li class="li2"><span class="s1"><strong>Light and information:</strong> as the fastest travelling substance, light carries information, for example in optical fibres. Pointing a laser into running water recreates this effect: as the water lights up, you see the information moving along, bending along the water stream. Light has its own language.</span></li>
<li class="li2"><span class="s1"><strong>Light and sound:</strong> light moves like water, in waves. <strong>Lissajous figures</strong> make sound waves visible, turning a simple laser into a message through vibrations, even those inaudible to people but perceived by other species.</span></li>
</ul>
<p class="p1"><span class="s1">Between 9 PM and 2 AM, the site stands empty. We keep the existing warehouse and offices and use this <strong>free time</strong> to introduce new movement, with light as the tool to regulate it. </span></p>
<p class="p1"><span class="s2">The boundary created by light exists only in how it is perceived. A human may see a threshold, a truck may see a signal, and an animal may not see it at all.&nbsp;<span class="s1">As on the forklift, light becomes a <strong>mediator of coexistence</strong>.</span></span></p>
