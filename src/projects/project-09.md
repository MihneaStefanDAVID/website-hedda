<p class="p1"><span class="s1"><strong>What remains of a monastery when the community that fills it with life disappears?</strong></span></p>
<p class="p1"><span class="s1">In March 2026, we spent a seminar week at the Dominican convent in Ilanz, living with the sisters, sharing their meals and following the rhythm of their daily life. Once at the center of the region's schools and hospitals, the community of around 60 sisters is growing older and has chosen to shape the transformation of the convent itself. The convent is understood not as a static monument, but as <strong>a lived place in transformation</strong>.</span></p>
<p class="p1"><span class="s1">After a crash course in documentary filmmaking with architect and filmmaker Adrian Dorschner, we worked in groups, each approaching a different side of the convent: daily life, liturgy, space and light, landscape, the archive. As part of the <em>Oral History</em> group, I filmed the sisters' <strong>personal life stories</strong> and their relationship to the spaces they live in. Listening to them changed our group&rsquo;s vision on how we view the monastery.</span></p>
<p class="p1"><span class="s1">The films remain <strong>fragmentary and contradictory</strong>, and that is how we experienced the place.</span></p>
<p class="p1"><span class="s1">Alongside the filming, we discussed with the curators of the ZAZ how such works can be shown in an exhibition, developing the scenographic concept together. The films are part of <a href="https://zaz-bellerieve.ch/"><span class="s2"><em>Out of Service? Swiss Sacred Architecture in Transition</em></span></a> at ZAZ Bellerive in Zurich from September 2026 to February 2027.</span></p>
