<p>The intention with this kitchen was to <strong>c</strong>reate a socialized form of living.&nbsp;Originally, kitchens were places where only women were expected to work, but they were still standardized to a man&rsquo;s body.</p>
<p><em><strong>Domesticity was seen as a female trait for a long time.</strong>&nbsp;</em></p>
<p>Since then, studies have helped kitchens become more optimized, but I wanted to go further. Why not also reduce waste by creating a communal kitchen? To&nbsp;<strong>create a space for communication, cooking, and living.</strong></p>
<p>The existing building houses a recycling company, a waste analysis company and a bike shop with two badminton courts. While the two companies are closed off and fenced, the bike shop opens directly onto the street, making it the most public and busiest space in the building.</p>
<p>The linoleum floor of the fits a kitchen perfectly because it is durable and easy to clean. Also, the lines of the two courts are already perfectly sized for kitchen counters.&nbsp;</p>
<p>The furniture is movable and adaptable, except for the sink, which stays in place like a statue. Water and electricity come from the upper beam, following the same path as the lighting. The tables are modular and can be moved around easily. They can be placed together to fit an entire apartment building for a meal, or they can be stored on the side. All of this happens while the badminton game is not interrupted.</p>
