<p>Do we live a house or do we house a life?&nbsp;</p>
<p>Living together always comes with conflicts, even if it means just living with yourself. <strong>Can you be your own family?</strong> The question of what makes a family, where it starts and where it ends, led us to the actors of our project: <em>the ITSELF</em>. Four characters, one person living with themselves at different ages, but at the same time: a kid, a young adult, an adult and a ghost. Imagine living with yourself: <strong>would there be more conflicts or less, would you need more privacy or less?</strong></p>
<p>Our fragment stands between two existing walls and asks: <strong>does remembrance need space?</strong> Some walls already existed, others are constantly being filled with life. The memories of the space that was there before won't just go away, just like the memories of the lives lived in it.</p>
<p>At the center of the fragment is <em>a rotating wall</em>, and it is always creating a conflict. To wash your hands in the sink, you have to move it. To turn it over the table, you have to clear the table first. Since electricity runs only through the wall, the lamps can only be plugged in where it stands. When you enter, the wall opens up either to the kitchen or to the other space, never to both. Even the shared bed has drawers that, once opened, stand in your way. Every movement of one character changes the space of the others.</p>
<p>Even though you know yourself so well, conflicts appear, and you have to find a balance to coexist with them. <strong>Having to take care of each other and look after yourself</strong> is at the heart of our concept: you need to act in a way that you are present and aware of the others.</p>
<p><strong>How does it end, and what is worth saving?</strong> Sometimes all that is left are some memories, so we concluded with a little memory collection of our moments and conflicts in this fragment.</p>
